Critical Challenges

Problems Facing Nepal's
Electricity Sector

Despite remarkable progress, Nepal's electricity infrastructure continues to grapple with deep-rooted structural challenges that affect millions of lives.

Load Shedding Legacy

Until 2016, Nepalis endured up to 14 hours of daily power cuts. The psychological and economic scars persist in business planning and public trust.

Impact Severity High

Seasonal Dependency

Dry season generation drops by 60%. Nepal imports over 500 MW from India during winter months, draining foreign reserves.

Impact Severity Critical

Rural Electrification Gap

7% of the population (approx. 2 million people) still lacks grid access, concentrated in remote Himalayan and Terai regions.

Impact Severity Moderate

Aging Infrastructure

40% of transmission lines are over 30 years old. Technical losses reach 15.5% — nearly double the global average of 8%.

Impact Severity High

High Electricity Costs

Industrial tariffs average NPR 12.50/kWh, among the highest in South Asia, discouraging manufacturing investment.

Impact Severity High

Cross-Border Dependency

Heavy reliance on Indian imports during dry months creates geopolitical vulnerability and trade imbalance concerns.

Impact Severity Moderate

Nepal's Energy Future

From massive hydropower projects to community solar, Nepal is deploying a multi-pronged strategy to achieve energy security.

Hydropower Expansion

Nepal has identified 83,000 MW of hydropower potential. Projects like Upper Tamakoshi (456 MW) and Arun-3 (900 MW) are transforming the generation landscape.

Historical Journey

The Road to Power

1911 Beginning

First Hydropower Plant

The 500 kW Pharping Hydropower Station became Nepal's first power plant, commissioned during the Rana era with British engineering support.

1985 Expansion

Kulekhani Reservoir

Nepal's only reservoir-type hydropower plant (92 MW) was built with Japanese assistance, providing crucial dry-season peaking power.

2008 Crisis

Peak Load Shedding

Power cuts reached 14-16 hours daily. The crisis spurred public demand for reform and accelerated hydropower development.

2016 Turnaround

Load Shedding Ends in Kathmandu

Thanks to new plants and improved management, the capital became load-shedding free — a symbolic milestone for the nation.

2021 Milestone

Upper Tamakoshi Goes Live

The 456 MW Upper Tamakoshi Hydropower Project — Nepal's largest domestically financed plant — began full commercial operation.

2023 Export Era

Nepal Becomes Net Exporter

For the first time, Nepal exported 452 MW to India during monsoon months, marking a historic shift from importer to exporter.

2024 Present

Nationwide 24/7 Power

Nepal achieved round-the-clock electricity supply nationwide. Total installed capacity crossed 2,700 MW with 83% from hydropower.
